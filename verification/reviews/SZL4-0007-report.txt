Density and separability
1. countable_monoid_closure concerns algebraic Submonoid.closure, not topological
   closure. It places each element in the range of finite lists in the
   countable generator subtype. The empty word supplies the unit; multiplication
   concatenates finite words. This is a valid countability argument.
2. closed_adjoin_separable does not claim that the complex linear span is a
   countable set. It uses countability of words in s union star(s), the actual
   Mathlib identity StarAlgebra.adjoin_eq_span, and IsSeparable.span. That pinned
   theorem uses separability of the scalar ring Complex, continuous addition,
   and continuous scalar multiplication. Passing to norm closure preserves
   separability in the ambient Cstar topology.
3. polynomial_inclusion_mem is proved algebraically, first extracting real
   and imaginary parts of the included edge from any star-subalgebra containing
   it, then inducting through the real free algebra and its complex tensor
   extension. No generation theorem is supplied as a premise.
4. generated_eq_top uses the genuine dense range of the polynomial inclusion
   into the separated completion, together with the norm-closed generated
   star-subalgebra. The resulting equality is for the actual GraphUniversal
   family, not an abstract generation predicate with assumed consequences.
5. separable uses finiteness of V and E to obtain countability of vertex/edge
   ranges, the preceding closed-adjoin result, and generated_eq_top. The proof
   does not use the path representation or assume it faithful. It asserts
   separability of the graph Cstar algebra, not of the possibly nonseparable
   path Hilbert space or its entire bounded-operator algebra.
6. DensityProbe compiles an actual countable dense subset, the assertion that
   every closed star-subalgebra containing all vertices/edges is top, and an
   explicit closure equality in the completion's topology.

The actual Hilbert space and operators
7. Hilbert X is lp (fun _ : X => Complex) 2, with its pinned Mathlib Hilbert
   norm and inner product. No finite matrix truncation or entrywise surrogate
   norm occurs. Bounded operators have the actual ContinuousLinearMap operator
   norm, star is the Hilbert adjoint, and CStarAlgebra is the standard complete
   Hilbert-space operator instance. Relevant Mathlib norm_adjoint_comp_self
   proves the Cstar identity for that norm.
8. coordinate is the canonical norm-preserving lp.single map. Distinct
   coordinates are orthogonal. inclusion f is the Hilbert-sum linear isometry
   formed from those coordinates for an injection f. The imported construction
   is a genuine summable Hilbert sum and proves norm preservation; it is not an
   assumed coordinate-reindexing operator.
9. inclusion_adjoint_apply derives coordinate restriction from the adjoint's
   inner-product identity. The in-range and out-of-range coordinate formulas
   follow from that result and the adjoint-isometry identity. rangeProjection
   is inclusion composed with its actual adjoint, with self-adjointness and
   idempotence proved. transport f g is inclusion g composed with the adjoint
   of inclusion f; its initial/final projections are established by composition.
10. operator_ext is justified by lp.ext_continuousLinearMap at exponent 2.
    The pinned Mathlib proof uses convergence of the sum of the coordinate
    vectors for p != infinity and continuity of the maps. Equality merely on
    algebraic finite-support vectors is not silently promoted to all vectors.

Paths, recursion, and all CK relations
11. Path source target consists of p : Nat -> E satisfying
       target(p n) = source(p(n+1)) for every n.
    head=p 0 and start=source(head). Prefixing e is allowed exactly when the
    old start equals target e. Tail/prefix inverse laws are proved on the
    actual subtype, including the composability witness.
12. The no-sinks hypothesis is exactly forall v, exists e, source e = v.
    exists_start chooses one outgoing edge per vertex, iterates the function
    v -> target(chosenEdge v), and uses those chosen edges to form an actual
    infinite path. The proof explicitly verifies composability and the start
    vertex. It needs neither a separately assumed path witness nor graph
    finiteness; classical choice is in the recorded standard axiom baseline.
13. Vertex v is the range projection of the injection of paths starting at v.
    Edge e transports that target-starting coordinate space to the range of
    prefixing e. Coordinate formulas establish that the adjoint removes the
    first edge precisely when it is e, and is zero otherwise.
14. Every CK clause was inspected:
    - each vertex is a self-adjoint idempotent;
    - distinct vertices are orthogonal since a path has only one start;
    - the finite vertex sum is the identity on every coordinate, hence on lp;
    - s_e* s_e is p_target(e), by the transport initial-projection identity;
    - distinct edge range projections are orthogonal since a path has one head;
      the actual initial-product orthogonality is then derived using the
      previously proved partial-isometry equivalence;
    - the outgoing finite range sum is p_v because a path starting at v has
      exactly one first edge with source v.
    family assembles these proved operator equations. No CK relation is assumed
    of arbitrary previously supplied operators. The source/target convention
    agrees with the manuscript and previously audited GraphRelations.
15. The outgoing formula holds even at sink vertices in this infinite-path
    representation, where the vertex is zero. This is compatible with the
    more general Family definition, which requires CK only at emitting
    vertices. General graphs with sinks are not claimed to have nonzero
    vertices in this representation. NoSinks is retained for nonzeroness.
16. vertex_ne_zero chooses a path starting at v and applies the alleged zero
    operator to its lp.single coordinate of coefficient one; evaluation at
    that coordinate gives the contradiction. Norm-one results then use actual
    Cstar identities and nonzero projections. No faithfulness assumption is
    used to obtain these concrete nonzero witnesses.

Universe and decision-instance scope
17. With V : Type u and E : Type v, Path, its lp Hilbert space, and its operator
    algebra all live in Type v; the start/composability predicates may mention
    Type u without enlarging the path carrier. SignatureProbe elaborates these
    universe equalities and the actual operator CStarAlgebra instance.
18. The path family is therefore a representation in GraphUniversal's target
    universe v. The exact conclusion is GraphUniversal.vertex.{v,u,v} != 0;
    the graph carrier itself is Type(max u v). The results do not assert
    independence of the representation universe or nonzeroness in an
    arbitrarily different fixed representation universe. For finite graph
    indices in Type0, they apply to the Type0 universal graph algebra.
19. Exact-source API finding: the original path module declares a local
    classical Decidable instance before its namespace. Its last three
    universal corollaries therefore use the induced fixed classical
    DecidableEq V, rather than quantifying over a user's supplied instance.
    Direct application to GraphUniversal.vertex with Unit's default instance
    produced an actual compiler type mismatch. This does not invalidate the
    mathematical theorem at its stated instance, but it prevents treating that
    original theorem as directly generic over decision instances.
20. The finding was reported to the coordinator before any repair proposal.
    DecisionTransportProbe proves the safe adapter with [d : DecidableEq V]:
    define d' a b := Classical.propDecidable(a=b), obtain d=d' by Subsingleton.elim,
    and use Eq.mpr (congrArg P hd) for the explicit proposition P depending on
    the decision instance. This transports the entire dependent graph object,
    norm, and statement, while leaving the representation universe unchanged.
    The release GraphNonzero contains these three proofs. NonzeroProbe then
    successfully applies the resulting API using Unit's ordinary default
    instance. The original fixed-instance statements remain byte-for-byte
    unchanged and retain their exact scope.
21. GraphNonzero.universal_edge_norm_one uses the actual Cstar square identity
    and nonzero initial vertex's norm one. nontrivial additionally requires
    Nonempty V, which is necessary: NonzeroProbe verifies that the empty graph
    satisfies NoSinks vacuously yet has a subsingleton universal algebra.

Substantive controls and limitations
22. PathProbe checks norm preservation, Hilbert-adjoint star, exact coordinate
    transport and annihilation, and the two-loop graph's actual Cuntz relations.
    It proves an edge has a proper range by testing on the other head's path
    coordinate, while its initial projection is identity and norm is one.
23. NegativeControlProbe proves that for the one-vertex/one-loop graph the
    concrete path edge is identity. A genuine Complex-valued family with edge
    -1 proves the universal edge is not identity. Consequently the canonical
    path lift is NOT injective for this graph. This confirms a material scope
    boundary: all vertex projections being nonzero does not by itself make the
    infinite-path representation faithful. Neither audited source claims it
    does. The density proof is independent of such a claim.
24. No graph simplicity, pure infiniteness, CPAP/nuclearity, graph/amalgam
    identification, K-theory, classification, or end-to-end Suzuki MainClaim is
    certified by this review. Those are separate obligations or explicit
    published inputs in the coordinator's subsequently revised objective.
